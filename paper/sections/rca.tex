\subsection{Anomaly Root Cause Analysis and the Prevention Feedback Loop}

The RCA module closes the governance loop by converting recurring divergence
patterns into policy suggestions. After each generation, \texttt{RootCauseAnalyzer}
queries historical incidents and groups them by workload type and incident
category: over-provisioned requests, idle cluster patterns, runaway jobs, and
undeclared token budget. When the same pattern recurs at least three times within
a lookback window, the module synthesizes a \texttt{Policy} object with confidence
proportional to pattern frequency and submits it to \texttt{PolicyRegistry}.

This mechanism matters for governance timing in a specific way. Pre-billing
intervention handles known divergence patterns through preconfigured rules, but
novel or low-frequency patterns are not covered by static policy. The feedback
loop fills that gap: workloads that escape pre-billing intervention, behave
anomalously at runtime, and are flagged by IFS eventually contribute to new
learned policies that catch the same pattern in subsequent submissions. The policy
learning convergence result from Exp~6 is partly attributable to this loop—Full
PBCP substantially outperforms the No Phase~3 ablation because learned policies
progressively reduce the baseline rate of uncaught divergence.

The current feedback mechanism has two honest limitations. First, it operates on
synthetic incident data. Incident classification in the benchmark is deterministic
and the pattern counts are controlled, so the confidence and frequency estimates
may not reflect the noisier, more ambiguous incident streams that appear in
real enterprise environments. Second, the current implementation does not weight
incidents by recency or cost severity within the pattern-counting logic. Two
incidents with high cost impact and three incidents with low cost impact are
treated identically, which could generate low-value learned policies while
missing high-priority intervention opportunities. Both limitations are addressable
through production integration and represent near-term extensions rather than
design defects.
